CALIFORNIA ADMISSIONS · APPLYING FALL 2026 FOR FALL 2027

The University of California doesn't use the Common App. A lot of what you've heard about college applications works differently here.

> **QUICK ANSWER**
>
> One application gets you into all nine UC schools — you just check a box for each one you want. There's no personal statement, no letters of recommendation, and no test scores. Instead, you write short answers to 4 out of 8 questions.
>
> The dates are fixed: the application opens August 1, but you can only submit it between October 1 and November 30, 2026. There's no early decision or early action — everyone applies in the same window, at the same time.

## Six things that work differently at UC schools

| Thing | Most other colleges | UC schools |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where you apply | The Common App, plus extra questions for each school | UC's own website. One form. You just check the box for each UC school you want. |
| Essays | One long essay (650 words), plus extra ones per school | 4 short answers, picked from 8 questions. Each one is 350 words max. |
| Letters of recommendation | Usually 1–3 required | Not required. UC actually tells you not to send them. |
| Test scores (SAT/ACT) | Some schools want them, most don't require them | Not used at all, even if you send one. |
| Deadlines | Different dates for early and regular applications | One deadline: October 1 to November 30. No early option anywhere in the system. |
| Your GPA | Your school's own GPA, from your transcript | UC calculates its own version of your GPA, using its own rules. It's often a different number than the GPA on your report card. |

## Key dates for fall 2027

| Date | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Aug 1, 2026 | The application opens. You can start filling it out now, even though you can't submit yet. This is a good time to start writing. |
| Oct 1 – Nov 30, 2026 | You can submit. Nov 30 is the deadline — don't plan around it getting extended. |
| Oct 1, 2026 | FAFSA and the California Dream Act Application open. This is where financial aid starts. |
| Mar 2, 2027 | Deadline for California state aid (Cal Grant) to get priority. |
| Mar 1–31, 2027 | You find out if you got in. |
| May 1, 2027 | Deadline to tell the school you picked that you're coming. |
| Jul 1, 2027 | Your final transcript needs to be sent to the school. |

## Five tips for writing your answers

- 350 words is short — a regular college essay gives you 650 words to build up to a point slowly. Here you have about half that, so get to the point early. Don't just cut down an essay you wrote for a different application — it usually shows.
- It doesn't matter which 4 questions you pick — UC says all 8 questions count equally. Picking a "better" question isn't a real strategy — picking the ones you can actually answer well is.
- Pick 4 questions that show different sides of you — if all four answers are basically the same story, it reads like one answer, not four.
- There's an "Additional Comments" box — it's not a 5th essay — it's meant to explain something specific, like a hard semester or a gap in your transcript. Don't use it to squeeze in more general writing about yourself.
- UC looks at what your school offered you — if your school only offers 3 AP classes and you took all 3, that counts as pushing yourself — even if another school down the street offers 15.

## A few more things to know

- Berkeley and Merced use semesters. The other seven UC schools use quarters. That changes how fast classes move and how often you'll have finals.
- Your UC GPA and your school GPA usually aren't the same number, because UC has its own rules for counting extra credit from honors and AP classes. Check UC's website for the exact current rules.
- Applying to more UC schools costs more money, but it doesn't improve your odds at any one school — each campus makes its own decision separately. Pick schools based on genuine fit, not on applying to as many as possible.
