COLLEGE ADMISSIONS · CLASS OF 2027 & 2028

It depends on which colleges are on your list. Here's how to check, fast.

> **QUICK ANSWER**
>
> At the most selective schools, yes. Six of the eight Ivy League schools now require a score. So do Stanford, MIT, and Caltech.
>
> At most other colleges, no. You get to choose whether to send a score.
>
> At every UC school — Berkeley, UCLA, San Diego, and the rest — they won't even look at your score if you send one.
>
> The rule isn't national. It's school by school. Check your own list against the table below.

## Four groups of schools

Colleges fall into one of four groups. Find your schools below.

| Group | What it means | Example schools |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requires a score | You must send a score. No score means an incomplete application. | Harvard, Yale, Penn, Brown, Dartmouth, Cornell, MIT, Caltech, Stanford, Georgetown, Purdue, UT Austin. Also the Florida, Georgia, and Tennessee public college systems. |
| Optional — for now | You can skip it this year. Starting next year (Class of 2028), it's required. | Princeton and Columbia |
| Optional | You choose. A good score can still help your application. | Most U.S. colleges |
| Won't look at it | You can send a score, but it won't be used at all. | All nine UC schools and the CSU schools |

## Three things worth knowing before you decide

- A good score today needs to be higher than it used to be — for the past few years, mostly strong test-takers sent scores to test-optional schools. That pushed the "good score" bar up. A score that looked strong at Harvard in 2019 may look average there now. If you're sending a score somewhere optional, check that school's current range first — don't guess from an old number.
- The ACT changed in 2025 — the Science section is now optional on the test itself. But Harvard, Yale, Penn, Brown, Dartmouth, and Cornell still want to see it. If any of those schools are on your list, take the ACT with Science included.
- A newer test called the CLT now exists — about 325 colleges accept it, including the University of Florida. In February 2026, the whole UNC system said they'll accept it too, starting fall 2027. Most students still don't need to think about this — but check if a school on your list is one of the ones that takes it.

## What should you actually do?

| If this is you… | Do this | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only applying to UC or CSU schools | Skip the SAT/ACT completely. | They won't use the score no matter how good it is. Spend that time on grades and your essays instead. |
| Applying to a mix (UC plus other schools) | Take it once, in the spring of junior year. | One test date keeps every school on your list open. You won't be able to change your mind later at an Ivy. |
| Hoping for a merit scholarship | Take the test, even at schools where it's optional. | Scholarship money often still depends on a score, even at schools where admission doesn't. At Alabama, for example, a low GPA now requires a score no matter what. |
| Got a good GPA but a rough first practice test | Look into "superscoring" before giving up. | Some schools (MIT, Yale, Purdue) combine your best scores from different test dates. A second try can fix just your weak section. |

## You don't have to pay to prep

Free official SAT practice exists through Khan Academy, built with the College Board. The CLT has its own free practice tests too. If you qualify for free or reduced-price lunch at school, you also qualify for a fee waiver — meaning the test itself is free, along with sending your scores to colleges. Paid prep classes cost $500 to $2,000 or more, and there isn't strong evidence they beat the free option for most students. Start with the free practice. Only pay for a course if it's fixing one specific, known weak spot.
