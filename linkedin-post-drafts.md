### Draft 2. The problem: access to good guidance

**Frame:** neutral, uses the access framing from [LI] post 3 with the reconciled numbers. **Audience:** parents, counselors, educators. **Pairs with:** the Post 3 graphic (corrected). **Length:** 1084 characters.

**Hook:** Good guidance was never scarce. Access to it was.

```
Good guidance was never scarce. Access to it was.

Picture two California students with the same grades at the same school. One family can pay for a private college counselor. Packages commonly run $4,000 to $12,000. The other leans on the school counselor.

That is not a story about bad counselors. The American School Counselor Association recommends 250 students per counselor, and high school counselors nationally carry about 195 to 224. California's often-quoted 432 includes elementary and middle schools.

The real limit is time. Public school counseling departments spend about 22% of their time on college admission counseling. At private schools it is about 51%.

So the gap is structural: how many hours of attention a family can reach, and what it costs to get them.

That is the gap we are working on at D2Excel: more ways for students and families to reach good guidance, and information that is not gated behind who can already afford it.

Sources: ASCA 2024-25 ratios (via EdSource and K-12 Dive); NACAC and ASCA on counselor time; published counseling price guides.
```

**Hook:** Before you pay a college counselor, get three things in writing.

```
Before you pay a college counselor, get three things in writing.

Private counseling packages commonly run $4,000 to $12,000, so it is worth asking before you commit. Three checks matter most, out of ten we cover in our guide:

1. Every fee and the refund policy, before work starts. In one 2025 survey of 600 independent counselors (self-selected, so a guide rather than a benchmark), 72% did not refund or transfer unused hours.
```
