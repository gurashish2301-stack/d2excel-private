## 1. The caseload gap (Template A)
The average US school counselor served 372 students in 2024-25.

That is nearly 50% above the 250 ASCA recommends, and it counts every grade, not only high school.

Picture that across a single school year. Every family needs a course schedule, a college list, aid forms and recommendation letters, often in the same few weeks. Now multiply all of that by 372. Even the most dedicated counselor runs out of hours before every family gets the time it deserves.

This isn't a counselor problem. It's a structural math problem.

D2Excel is building an open marketplace connecting students and families to a wider range of independent counselors, plus decision-support information that isn't gated behind who can afford it.

Source: ASCA 2024-25 student-to-counselor ratio data, released Feb 2026 and reported by K-12 Dive.

## 2. No counselor at school (Template D)
About 1 in 6 US public high schools had no school counselor.

That is federal data for 2020-21, a pandemic year, and it affected about 4% of high school students. Here, "high school" means any school with a grade 9 to 12.

Picture one of those students. There is no school counselor down the hall to explain college lists, aid deadlines or which courses colleges expect. Their family can still work it out, but with one less person to lean on.

This isn't a motivation problem. It's an access problem.

D2Excel is building to close that gap: an open marketplace linking students and families to a wider range of independent counselors, plus decision-support information that isn't gated behind who can afford it.

Source: US Department of Education Office for Civil Rights, Civil Rights Data Collection, 2020-21.

## 3. The cost of getting it wrong (Template B)
About 37% of students who started college in fall 2008 transferred at least once within six years.

That is the National Student Clearinghouse count for that cohort, and it includes any change of institution. Some are planned and positive; others follow a poor fit.

Picture a student who chose a school on name, distance and a campus tour. By spring it did not fit, so they transferred. Some credits may not carry over, and the family still pays in tuition, time and momentum.

This isn't a commitment problem. It's a decisions-without-information problem.

That is what D2Excel is building for: an open marketplace connecting students and families to a wider range of independent counselors, plus decision-support information that isn't gated behind who can afford it.

Source: National Student Clearinghouse Research Center, Signature Report 9, July 2015 (fall-2008 cohort).

## 4. The time problem (Template C)
Public school counselors spend 22% of their time on college counseling. In private schools, it is 51%.

NACAC's figures also put the average public school caseload at 405 students (2021-22). Counselors are doing essential work with that time, from scheduling to student well-being. There are just more jobs than hours.

Put differently: a little over one hour in five. For a family, that can mean one short meeting to cover a college list, an aid form and a deadline calendar, with little room left to revisit it.

This isn't an effort problem. It's a time problem.

D2Excel is building an open marketplace to connect students and families with a wider range of independent counselors, along with decision-support information that isn't gated behind who can afford it.

Source: NACAC school counseling data, 2021-22 caseload year.

## 5. Pell Grants left unclaimed (Template D)
The class of 2024 left about $4.4 billion in Pell Grants unclaimed.

California and Texas each topped $500 million. Pell Grants are federal grants for students with financial need.

Picture a family that wasn't sure it would qualify, or what the form was really asking, and had no one to ask.

There's good news too. More than 70% of California seniors in the class of 2026 completed the FAFSA or CADAA (California's Dream Act application), a record.

This isn't a willingness problem. It's a navigation problem.

D2Excel is building toward a clearer path: an open marketplace connecting students and families to a wider range of independent counselors, plus decision-support information that isn't gated behind who can afford it.

Source: NCAN, April 2025, for the class of 2024; EdSource, June 2026, citing the California Student Aid Commission.

## 6. First-generation students (Template A)
In 2012-13, 72% of first-generation students met with a counselor about college. For students with a bachelor's-degree parent, it was 82%.

That is a 10-point gap, from 2012-13, so read it as a marker, not today's exact figure.

Picture two equally curious students in the same school. One goes home to a parent who has been through the process and knows which questions to ask. The other is the first in the family to try, learning the system and the questions at once.

This isn't an ambition problem. It's a head-start problem.

D2Excel is building with those students in mind: an open marketplace connecting students and families to a wider range of independent counselors, plus decision-support information that isn't gated behind who can afford it.

Source: National Center for Education Statistics, NCES 2023-097, Aug 2023 (2012-13 data).
